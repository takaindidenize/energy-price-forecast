# Out-of-sample check on 2026, and more data for Turkey

Generated by `analysis/turkey_2026.py`. MAE in EUR/MWh. All forecasts are kept within the market's
price limits. 2026 runs from 1 January to 04 November.

## Do the 2025 results hold?

| Period | Mean price | MAE | MAE / mean price | rMAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Germany-Luxembourg 2025 | 89.3 | 15.9 | 17.8% | 0.48 |
| Germany-Luxembourg 2026 | 115.8 | 22.2 | 19.2% | 0.51 |
| Turkey 2025 | 58.9 | 5.9 | 10.1% | 0.65 |
| Turkey 2026 | 39.0 | 9.4 | 24.1% | 0.77 |

rMAE is the MAE relative to the seasonal naive on the same hours; lower is better.

## Does more data help in Turkey?

| Model | MAE 2025 | MAE 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| LEAR | 5.94 | 9.40 |
| LEAR + load plan | 5.87 (t = 2.7) | 9.57 (t = -5.0) |
| LEAR + generation mix | 5.94 (t = -0.2) | 9.14 (t = 3.8) |
| LEAR + both | 5.88 (t = 1.4) | 9.19 (t = 2.8) |
| LightGBM | 6.11 | 9.04 |
| LightGBM + both | 6.05 (t = 0.8) | 8.95 (t = 0.6) |

The t statistic compares daily errors with the same model without the extra data; above about 2,
the extra data is reliably better, below about -2, reliably worse. The load plan is an upper
bound (see the script's docstring). Neither source helps consistently across both years, so
neither was added to the model.

## Does a shorter training window help?

| Model | de_lu 2025 | de_lu 2026 | tr 2025 | tr 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LEAR, all history | 15.88 | 22.22 | 5.94 | 9.40 |
| LEAR, last 365 days | 16.02 | 22.33 | 6.06 | 9.48 |
| LEAR, last 180 days | 17.83 | 26.01 | 6.25 | 9.28 |
| LightGBM, all history | 17.64 | 25.52 | 6.11 | 9.04 |

The 180-day LEAR is unstable: starting the weekly refits a few days later moves its German 2026
MAE from 26.0 to 24.1, while the other models barely change.
